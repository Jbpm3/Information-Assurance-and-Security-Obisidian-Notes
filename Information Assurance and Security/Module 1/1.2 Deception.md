## 1.2.1 Social Engineering
- a non-technical strategy that attempts to manipulate individuals into performing certain actions or divulging confidential information

**Pretexting**
![[pretexting.png]]
- occurs ==when an individual lies to gain access== to privileged data

**Something for something (quid pro quo)**
![[qpq.png]]
- involve a ==request for personal information in exchange for something,== like a gift

**Identity fraud**
![[identity fraud.png]]
- use of a ==person's stolen identity== to obtain goods or services by deception.


---
## 1.2.2 Social Engineering Tactics

**Authority**
- people are ==more likely to comply== when instructed by an ==authority figure==

**Intimidation**
- cybercriminals will often ==bully a victim== into taking an action that compromises security.

**Consensus**
- often called *social proof*
- work because ==people tend to act in the same way as other people== around them.

**Scarcity**
- people tend to act when they think there is a ==limited quantity of something available.==

**Urgency**
- people tend to act when they think ==there is a limited time to do so.==

**Familiarity**
- people are more likely to do what another person asks ==if they like this person.==

**Trust**
- Building trust in a relationship with a victim may require more time to establish.

## 1.2.6 Shoulder Surfing and Dumpster Diving

**Shoulder surfing**
![[shoulder surfing.png]]
- observing or literally ==looking over a target's shoulder== to gain valuable information
- can use binoculars or security cameras

**Dumpster diving**
![[dumpster diving.png]]
- ==going through a target's trash== to see what information has been thrown out
- documents containing sensitive information should be shredded or stored in burn bags.

## 1.2.7 Impersonation and Hoaxes

**Impersonation**
![[impersonation.png]]
- act of tricking someone into doing something by ==pretending to be someone else.==

**Hoaxes**
![[hoaxes.png]]
-  ==act intended to deceive or trick someone==
- can cause just as much disruption as an actual security breach

---

## 1.2.8 Piggybacking and Tailgating

Occurs when a criminal follows an authorized person to gain physical entry into a secure location or a restricted area.

 Criminals can achieve this by:
- Giving the appearance of being escorted into the facility by an authorized person.
- Joining and pretending to be part of a large crowd that enters the facility.
- Targeting an authorized person who is careless about the rules of the facility.

## 1.2.9 Other Methods of Deception

**Invoice Scam**
- ==fake invoices== are sent with the goal of receiving money from a victim.
- prompts them to put their credentials into a fake login screen
- may also include urgent or threatening language

**Watering hole attack**
- an attacker ==observes or guesses== what websites an organization uses most often
- infects one or more of them with malware.

**Typosquatting**
- ==relies on typos== made by individuals when inputting a website address into their browser.
- the incorrect URL will bring the individuals to a legitimate-looking website owned by the attacker

**Prepending**
- removes the 'external' email tag used by organizations
- warns the recipient that an email has originated from an external source
- tricks individual into ==believing that a malicious email was sent from inside their organization==

**Influence campaigns**
- often used in cyberwarfare
- very well coordinated and blend various methods such as *fake news*, *disinformation campaigns*, and *social media posts*
